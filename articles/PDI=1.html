Why is it hard to synthesize polymers with PDI close to 1 when nature does it effortlessly?

May 2026

PDI (Polymer dispersity Index) is a measure of the distribution of molecular mass in a given polymer sample. A PDI of 1 indicates that all polymer chains are of the same length, which is often desired for specific applications. However, achieving a PDI close to 1 in synthetic polymers is challenging due to various factors such as chain transfer reactions, termination events, and the inherent randomness in polymerization processes. In contrast, nature has evolved highly efficient enzymatic systems that can control polymerization with remarkable precision, allowing for the production of polymers with very low dispersity.

One valid argument could be that nature has had millions of years to evolve and optimize these processes, while synthetic methods are relatively new and still being refined. Additionally, natural polymerization often occurs under mild conditions and in highly controlled environments, which can be difficult to replicate in a laboratory setting.

But how exactly does nature achieve this level of control? Enzymes, which are biological catalysts, play a crucial role in guiding the polymerization process. They can selectively bind to monomers and facilitate their addition to the growing polymer chain in a highly regulated manner.
